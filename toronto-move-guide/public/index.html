What comes first?

Choose your moving strategy

If you need your sale proceeds for the purchase, Tsering generally recommends selling first. Base your next purchase on a conservative selling price.

  1. Get payout quotes and price your home conservatively.
  2. Negotiate enough time to find and close on your next home.
  3. Line up temporary housing and storage in case dates don’t match.

Tax sources and estimate assumptions

The model is for typical resale homes, using standard Ontario and City of Toronto rates reviewed November 2026. New-build HST, foreign-buyer taxes and special exemptions require a separate quote. Enter any applicable additional amount under other purchase costs. Toronto tax applies inside the City of Toronto, including its former municipalities.

Commission HST is calculated separately. Include applicable tax in your other cost estimates and use a signed amount for closing adjustments. The mortgage payment uses a Canadian fixed-rate convention with semiannual compounding. The insured estimate uses a standard 25-year-or-shorter owner-occupied premium and capitalizes it, with Ontario tax due separately.

Deposit funds are a subset of your liquid cash. Enter cash before any deposit already paid, or add the paid amount back to today’s balance. Include only funds reliably available when needed. Bridge interest is a simple daily estimate. The model treats bridge interest and fees as payable after the sale and excludes temporary housing unless you enter it under other purchase costs. Questions to ask your lender and lawyer
Mortgage payout and portability
Ask for a dated payout statement and a written comparison of breaking or porting the mortgage, including any penalty, discharge fee and portability window.
Qualification, deposit and HELOC
Confirm approval for the specific property and appraisal. Ask which funds are acceptable for the deposit and how any HELOC draw affects qualification and payout.
Bridge financing
Ask whether you need a firm sale, how much is available, when funds can advance and what happens if the sale is delayed.
Freehold and condo
Budget for a freehold inspection and near-term repairs. For a condo, have your lawyer review the status certificate, reserve fund, fees, assessments and moving rules.
